## Authorship and the use of AI

This project was conceived, directed and verified by a human author. An AI
research assistant was used as a tool during development. Because journals
(arXiv, COPE, ICMJE, Nature) do not permit AI systems to hold authorship — an
author must be able to take responsibility and give consent — the AI is credited
in the acknowledgements and methods, not as an author.

**What the AI did not do**

The AI did not select the research question, did not decide what counted as an
acceptable result, and did not adjudicate failures. Where its hypotheses were
tested and rejected — the physics-feature experiment and the label-curation
experiment in §5.4 — the rejections are reported. The AI's initial reading of the
evidence was that better feature representation would raise accuracy; the
measurement showed otherwise, and the study reports the measurement.

**Why this is declared openly**

Undisclosed AI use is the leading cause of retraction and reputational damage in
2025–2026. Declaring the tool, the role and the verification procedure is now
standard practice (ICMJE recommendations; COPE position statement on AI tools).
A reader is entitled to know which parts of a result were produced by a model
and which were verified by a human.
